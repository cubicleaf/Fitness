# Marcus Chen — The Seed Data Character

## The Basics

Marcus Chen, 29, software developer at a mid-size fintech company in East London. Born in Manchester to a Cantonese-speaking father and a Welsh mother. 5'10", currently 81kg/178lbs (down from 87kg seven months ago). Lives alone in a one-bedroom flat in Hackney. His gym is a no-frills basement place called "Iron Church" — ten-minute walk from his flat, open 24 hours, no classes, no smoothie bar. Just racks, dumbbells, and a few cable machines. He pays £35/month.

## Seven Months Ago (Early October 2025)

Marcus hadn't touched a weight in over two years. His last stint in the gym ended when COVID lockdowns scrambled his routine and he never rebuilt it. In the intervening years, he'd become the cliché: desk job, Deliveroo for dinner, the odd Sunday walk that he'd mentally classify as "cardio." He wasn't dramatically unfit — he could still climb stairs — but he'd lost all the muscle he'd built in his early twenties, his posture had degraded from twelve-hour coding sessions, and he'd developed a persistent ache in his right shoulder that his GP attributed to "desk work and weak rotator cuffs."

The catalyst was embarrassingly mundane: he tried to help a friend move a sofa up two flights of stairs and had to stop halfway, genuinely worried he might drop it. He was twenty-nine years old and struggling to carry furniture. That evening he signed up at Iron Church.

## Starting Place (October 13, 2025)

When he walked in for his first session on a Monday in mid-October, his numbers were humbling for someone who once benched 100kg in university:

- Bench Press: 132lbs (60kg) for shaky sets of 5 — noted "First session in 2 years. Humbling."
- Squat: barely hitting parallel at 154lbs (70kg)
- Barbell Rows: 132lbs (60kg) with terrible form
- Overhead Press: 88lbs (40kg) — shoulder clicking immediately
- Couldn't do a single unassisted pullup
- Plank: collapsed at 25 seconds
- Treadmill: gassed after 8 minutes of jogging

His right shoulder clicked during overhead presses (he noted it that first day). His hip flexors were so tight from years of sitting that his squat looked like a forward lean. He had essentially no mind-muscle connection — just moving weight from A to B with whatever muscles decided to fire.

Those first two weeks he kept it simple: 2-3 exercises per session, just learning the movements again. Bench, OHP, and a lateral raise. Rows, pulldowns, and curls. Squats and RDLs. Nothing fancy — just showing up.

## The Approach

Marcus is a developer, so naturally he over-researched everything. He spent a week reading about Push/Pull/Legs splits, progressive overload, and the importance of not doing too much too soon. He settled on a loose PPL framework: Push on Monday and Friday, Pull on Tuesday and Saturday, Legs on Wednesday, with Thursday and Sunday as rest days. In practice, he rarely follows this perfectly — some weeks he only manages three sessions, some weeks he shifts days around based on energy or work deadlines. His actual average across the full dataset: 3.5 sessions per week.

He doesn't follow a rigid program. He picks exercises in the moment based on what equipment is free and how his body feels. But he has clear favourites: Bench Press and Overhead Press on push days, Barbell Rows and Lat Pulldowns on pull days, Squats and Romanian Deadlifts on leg days. He throws in accessory work — curls, lateral raises, tricep pushdowns, dips — when he has time and energy. By week 3-4 he was running 4-5 exercises per session; by December, full 5-7 exercise sessions were normal.

## Phase 1 — Weeks 1-4 (Oct 13 - Nov 9): The Awkward Beginning

Short sessions. Lots of form notes. Low energy tags everywhere. He's relearning how to move under load. His shoulder clicks during every overhead press. He forgets to warm up. He skips his first scheduled leg day and notes it with guilty honesty: "Skipped legs this week. Again."

By the end of this phase, he's added Dips, Tricep Pushdowns, Lat Pulldowns, Walking Lunges, and Calf Raises to his rotation. The weights haven't moved much — he's focused on not hurting himself.

## Phase 2 — Weeks 5-8 (Nov 10 - Dec 7): Finding a Rhythm

This is where consistency kicks in. 3-4 sessions per week, almost every week. He reluctantly starts core work — Planks (starting around 28 seconds, hating every one), Cable Woodchops, Hanging Leg Raises. Core happens once a week at best.

His first treadmill run (early December): 8 minutes and he's gassed. He notes it: "First run in ages. Gassed after 8 minutes." He starts doing sporadic cardio — a treadmill run or jump rope session once every week or two.

Weights are moving up now: Bench from 132 to ~136lbs, Squat from 154 to ~175lbs (squat is where he sees the fastest gains — his legs had the most untapped potential). The "Felt Strong" tag starts appearing in his notes for the first time.

## Phase 3 — Weeks 9-12 (Dec 8 - Jan 4): Getting Serious

His NHS physio appointment finally comes through (6-week wait from his GP referral). The physiotherapist gives him PT exercises for his right shoulder: Face Pulls, Band Pull Aparts, and Dead Hangs. Told to do them 2-3 times per week at the end of pull sessions. He starts around late January in the data — he procrastinated a couple of weeks before actually doing them. His note on the first Face Pulls session: "First PT exercise."

The physio also flags his hip flexor tightness and thoracic spine mobility. Marcus adds Hip Flexor Stretches, Pigeon Stretches, and Thoracic Spine Rotations to his repertoire — sporadically, about once every two weeks, scattered through the data as standalone stretch/mobility sessions.

By mid-December, he notes: "Shoulder clicking less since starting PT exercises." Not gone, but improving.

Christmas break: Dec 24-26, no training. No data for those three days. Back at it Dec 27 or 29. His Dec 31 session gets a "New Year session" note — pushing through a bench session to close out the year.

## Phase 4 — Weeks 13-16 (Jan 5 - Feb 1): The Band Chapter + The Cold

**The Banded Pullup Journey** — this is the storyline Marcus cares about most. He can't do an unassisted pullup, hasn't been able to since university, and he's determined to get there. He started with a thick green band (provides roughly 25kg of assistance) on January 5. His initial sets: 8-7-6 reps, with the band doing significant work.

Green band progression over January: 8 → 9 → 10 → 11 reps on set 1, building steadily.

**The Cold (Jan 14-20):** Marcus came down with a nasty cold mid-January. Not COVID (he tested), just a proper British winter cold. He managed one session on Jan 20 — banded pullups dropped to 8 reps with the green band, noted "Still fighting cold." He took days off (no data for Jan 17-19). By Jan 25 he was back to normal.

By late January: Green band 11-10-9 — his best numbers before the switch.

**February 1st: The Red Band.** He graduated to the red band (roughly 15kg assistance). His note: "Graduated to red band! Less assistance. Feels real." Rep count reset to 11-10-9 — same numbers, less help. Progress.

## Phase 5 — Weeks 17-20 (Feb 2 - Feb 27): Building Momentum

Red band progression: 11 → 12 → 13 → 14 reps on set 1 over four weeks. His February 11th note: "Getting closer to unassisted." He thinks he might have one clean rep in him by late March. His February 27th session is a pull day. 14-13-12 with the red band. His note: "Five months in. This is who I am now."

All compounds trending up. Bench at 149lbs, Squat at 206lbs, Barbell Rows at 205lbs. Core improving (Plank up to ~72 seconds). Treadmill up to ~27 minutes. The cardio and mobility work is still sporadic — it's a known weakness — but it exists in the data.

## Phase 6 — Weeks 21-22 (Feb 28 - Mar 15): The Stride and the Setback

Marcus is hitting his stride. The weights are solid, the consistency is unbreakable. But he's also beginning to add recovery work intentionally — sauna sessions after heavy push and pull days, the occasional cold plunge in early March (once the weather warmed up). He's reading recovery advice, thinking longer term now.

**Early March chaos:** Marcus tries running outside for the first time since October. Friday, March 7, the weather's finally nice. He manages a short 15-minute jog (equivalent to about 900 seconds of treadmill work, but harder on the joints). It feels good. He's not a runner, but it's another dimension of fitness he's trying.

**The Rough Patch (March 8-10):** Sleep goes south. Construction noise from a neighboring flat (something about a renovation project) wakes him at 2am, 4am, 5am. He's getting 3-4 hours instead of his usual mediocre 5-6. By March 8, a Saturday pull session, his mood is a 1 in the notes: "Worst session in weeks. Construction noise all night." Pullups drop to 13 reps on set 1 (from 14). Rows drop to 201lbs from 208. He's not injured, just exhausted.

He debates skipping the next few days entirely but decides to push through. March 9 (Sunday) is just foam rolling and stretching — a light recovery day. March 10 (Monday) is a lighter push session, all weights down 5-10lbs, mood a 2. He's not ego-lifting. He's listening to his body.

But then something shifts.

**The Recovery and the PR (March 11-15):**

By Tuesday, March 11, Marcus is sleeping through the night again (the construction crew finally wrapped). His body responds immediately. Pull session: Banded pullups back to 16 reps on set 1. Barbell rows at 209lbs. Mood 5. The note says: "Bouncing back strong."

March 12 is a strong legs day — his best leg workout in weeks. Squat at 213lbs for 8 reps on set 1 (up from 7 the previous week). Mood 5. He's not thinking about that rough patch anymore.

And then Friday, March 14: **He hits a new all-time Bench Press PR of 175lbs.** It's a moment he's been thinking about since February. His note is emphatic: "HIT 175! New bench press PR!" Mood 5. The session feels perfect — every exercise flows, the weight moves cleanly. He's now officially 80% of the way to his goal of benching his bodyweight (180lbs).

March 15 (Saturday) closes the week with another strong pull session. Banded pullups at 16 reps. His note: "Almost ready for unassisted." He genuinely believes it now — he's maybe 3-4 weeks from one clean, unassisted pullup.

## Phase 7 — Weeks 23-25 (Mar 16 - Apr 5): The Approach

Marcus enters a golden stretch. His physio follow-up on March 20 goes well — shoulder cleared for heavier overhead pressing, the clicking has essentially disappeared. The PT exercises (Face Pulls, Band Pull Aparts, Dead Hangs) have done their job.

The banded pullup numbers are absurd now: 17, 18, 19 reps on set 1 with the red band. The band is barely assisting at the top of the movement. He knows it.

**March 24: First unassisted attempt.** He grabs the bar without a band for the first time since university. Gets halfway up and stalls. His note: "Tried one without the band. Got halfway up and stalled. Not there yet but closer than expected."

**March 31: Second attempt.** Chin reaches bar height but he can't hold it. The note: "Chin got to bar height but couldn't hold it. SO CLOSE. Maybe next week."

Meanwhile, his running is improving — he hits 30 minutes continuous on March 22 (a Sunday treadmill run, the first time he's ever run that long). On April 5, he manages 35 minutes outdoors. The 5K goal is starting to feel realistic.

Bench working weight climbs from 155 to 162. OHP gets a rep PR at 112lbs (7 reps). The red band pullups hit 19 on set 1 on April 4, and his note says it all: "19 reps on set 1. The band is barely helping. Tuesday I try again."

## Phase 8 — Weeks 26-27 (Apr 6-19): Breakthrough

**April 7, 2026. Tuesday. Pull day.** Marcus warms up extra. Nervous energy. After Barbell Rows and Lat Pulldowns, he walks to the pull-up bar. No band. No kipping. Just him and gravity.

**One clean rep.** Chin over the bar. Controlled descent. He stood staring at the bar for a full minute afterward.

His note: "ONE CLEAN REP. Chin over the bar. No kipping. No band. Six months and one day since I started. Stood there staring at the bar for a full minute after."

Six months and one day since he walked into Iron Church unable to carry a sofa up stairs.

**April 11:** One clean rep again. Tried for two and stalled at the top.

**April 14:** TWO clean reps on set one. One on set two. The note: "This is real now."

**April 18:** Two on set one AND set two. Almost cracked three.

By mid-April, Marcus is doing unassisted pullups at the start of every pull session, then switching to banded work for volume. The red band numbers are still climbing (20+ reps), but they're almost beside the point now. The real training is happening without the band.

## Phase 9 — Weeks 28-29 (Apr 20 - May 1): The Double

**April 20: Bench PR attempt — 178lbs.** Two pounds short of bodyweight. Sets of 5-4-3. His note: "Two pounds from bodyweight. So close." The working weight is now 163-164lbs.

**April 21: THREE unassisted pullups on set one.** Two weeks after his first ever rep. His note captures the absurdity: "Two weeks ago I could barely do one. The strength was always there, just needed to unlock it."

**April 27, 2026: The Bodyweight Bench.** 180 pounds. 4-3-2 reps. Marcus Chen benches his own bodyweight for the first time since university. His note: "180 POUNDS. BODYWEIGHT BENCH. First pullup three weeks ago and now this. Six and a half months."

**April 28:** Three unassisted pullups on both top sets. Four is the next target.

**April 30:** A strong leg session closes out April. Squat at 222lbs — over 100kg, a number that seemed impossible in October.

**May 1:** A working push session. Bench at 165 (working weight, not PR), all accessories at their highest numbers yet. His final note of the dataset: "Seven months tomorrow. Started at 132lbs bench and zero pullups. Now benching bodyweight and doing sets of three unassisted."

## Strengths

- **Consistent enough**: 3-4 sessions per week across 127 training days (7 months). Not perfect, but enough to make serious progress.
- **Patient with the process**: Doesn't ego-lift. Adds weight in small increments (2-3lbs per week on compounds). Accepts that he's rebuilding from zero.
- **Good at logging**: The act of tracking every set keeps him honest and motivated. He loves seeing the numbers tick up. His consistency with the app is unshakeable.
- **Willing to do the boring stuff**: Face pulls, dead hangs, stretching. He doesn't enjoy them, but he does them.
- **Adaptive to setbacks**: When sleep deprivation hit in early March, he didn't panic or overtrain. He took a light day and bounced back immediately.
- **Goal-oriented**: The pullup goal drove months of disciplined band work. The bench goal drove careful progressive overload. Both achieved.

## Weaknesses

- **Skips legs more often than he admits**: His pull and push sessions are more frequent and more enthusiastic. Leg days sometimes get bumped to "next week."
- **Poor sleep habits**: Stays up coding until 1-2am regularly. Gets 5-6 hours most nights. He knows this limits his recovery.
- **Nutrition is mediocre**: He eats enough protein (loosely targets 140g/day) but his overall diet is inconsistent. Some days it's chicken and rice, other days it's a kebab at midnight.
- **Avoids direct core work**: Planks bore him. Core appears in the data about once a week, and even that's generous.
- **Mobility procrastinator**: Stretch/mobility sessions appear in the data about once every two weeks. He knows he should do more. He doesn't.

## The Seed Data — What It Showcases

The CSV is designed to exercise every feature of the app across a robust 7-month dataset:

- **7 categories**: push, pull, legs, core, cardio, mobility, other (sauna, foam rolling, cold plunge)
- **30+ exercises** across all categories, including recovery modalities
- **3 weight types**: lbs (barbells/dumbbells/cables), bodyweight, banded
- **Reps and timed exercises**: compounds and accessories use reps; planks, dead hangs, stretches, sauna, cold plunge use duration in seconds
- **Session notes**: mood tags (1-5 scale), form cues (Form:Keep chest up), general notes (General:HIT 175! New bench press PR!)
- **Banded exercise notes**: band color as first pipe element (Red|Mood:5|Form:Smooth reps|General:...)
- **Per-day splits** (__DAYSPLIT__ rows): every training day has a split assignment matching its actual content
- **Weekly template** (__SPLITS__ row): Sun=Rest, Mon=Push, Tue=Pull, Wed=Legs, Thu=Rest, Fri=Push, Sat=Pull
- **Progression**: visible upward trends on all compounds with realistic variance
- **Regression**: the cold (Jan 14-20) and sleep deprivation (Mar 8-10) both show clear drops with recovery
- **Band transition**: Green → Red on Feb 1 with rep count reset, then steady progression to unassisted
- **Unassisted pullup journey**: failed attempts → first rep → progressive sets over 3 weeks
- **Two major PRs**: Bench 175 (Mar 14) and Bodyweight Bench 180 (Apr 27)
- **Recovery modalities**: Sauna sessions, cold plunges, foam rolling integrated throughout
- **Cardio progression**: 8-minute treadmill → 40-minute outdoor jogs
- **Gaps**: Christmas break (Dec 24-26), cold days off (Jan 17-19), light recovery days, sporadic rest days
- **Life happens**: Construction noise (Mar 8), sleep deprivation, illness, bouncing back from adversity

## The Bigger Picture

Marcus doesn't want to be a powerlifter or a bodybuilder. He wants to be the version of himself that can carry a sofa up stairs, that doesn't wake up with a sore back, that looks reasonable with his shirt off at the beach in August. He's rebuilding a foundation that he let crumble, and he's doing it alone, one set at a time, in a basement gym in East London.

Seven months in, he's accomplished more than he expected. Both of his original goals — the unassisted pullup and the bodyweight bench — are done. Not "almost" or "working toward." Done. The pullup came first (April 7), the bench three weeks later (April 27). He's now doing sets of three on pullups and thinking about five. He's squatting over 100kg and running for 40 minutes.

The data in his app — over 2,200 rows across 127 sessions over seven months — is the proof that he's actually showing up. And more importantly, it shows that consistency works. Even with setbacks (the cold in January, the construction noise in early March), the trend line goes up. That's all he needs.
